Phishing Indicators Identified:

1. Suspicious Sender Email Address:
The email claims to be from Google Notifications, but the sender's email address uses the domain webnotifications.net instead of an official Google domain such as **google.com**. This is a strong indication of phishing.

2. Brand Impersonation:
The email is designed to look like a legitimate Google Drive sharing notification. Attackers often imitate trusted brands to gain the victim's trust and increase the chances of interaction.

3. Social Engineering Tactics:
The message states that there have been changes to the company management structure and encourages the recipient to open the attached document. This creates curiosity and a sense of importance, increasing the likelihood that the recipient will click the attachment.

4. Suspicious Attachment:
The email contains an attachment named:

"Updated Company Org Chart - Contoso Corp.pdf"

Attackers frequently use attachments to deliver malware or redirect users to malicious websites. Attachments from unknown or unverified sources should always be treated with caution.

5. Domain Mismatch:
The sender claims to represent Google, but the email originates from a completely different domain. Legitimate Google Drive notifications are typically sent from official Google-owned domains.

6. Unknown Recipient Context:
The email is sent to a generic business account and references a company document without providing sufficient context or verification. This is a common phishing technique used to target employees within organizations.

Risk Assessment:
Risk Level: High
Reason:
This email impersonates a trusted service (Google Drive) and attempts to persuade the recipient to open a potentially malicious attachment. Such emails are commonly used to distribute malware, steal credentials, or gain unauthorized access to corporate systems.

Recommended Actions:

1. Do not open the attachment.
2. Verify the sender through official communication channels.
3. Report the email to the security team.
4. Delete the email if confirmed malicious.
5. Scan any downloaded files with antivirus software.
6. Educate users about phishing and social engineering attacks.

Conclusion:
The analyzed email exhibits multiple phishing characteristics, including sender spoofing, brand impersonation, suspicious attachments, and social engineering tactics. The sender domain does not match the organization it claims to represent, making the email highly suspicious. Users should avoid interacting with such emails and report them to the appropriate security team for investigation.
